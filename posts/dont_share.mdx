It seems like every day I see someone launching a new project that is clearly vibe coded. And to be clear, I don't really have anything against vibe coding.

What I do have something against, more spiritually than anything, is someone launching a new project just to launch a new project. Something that they clearly dont have any interest in working on. It usually follows the same pattern too.

Someone launches a project that they have been working on, in earnest, and it gets a lot of attention.

We get it. You pointed claude at instinct and said "make me this, make no mistakes". I can do that too. But everytime you do that, it waters down your reputation. You look like someone who just so desperately wants attention. You just want to be loved. So you're willing to spend your precious tokens to copy what someone else has already worked on.

That's how I know you don't love it. You don't love building, you just want the attention.

The audience can tell when you've built something that you're proud of. Something that you've dedicated real time and energy to working on. It doesn't matter if you wrote the code by hand or had an agent do it. It's not about the mechanics of how the product was built, it's about the sacrifice that you put into building it.

Committment is the new builder currency.

Before LLMs, when someone announced a project, it was clear that they had spent time and energy working on a project because there was no other way to actually build the project. The audience recognized this and, as a result, they got a baseline level of respect, if not also traction, as payment for their effort and commitment.

Said differently, creation was expensive.

But that transaction has been intermediated. Creation has been cheapend.

And as the laws of supply and demand hold true, when the cost of producing an widget decreases, the supply of that widget increases and the price drops.

And, in a market with faceless participants selling their widgets that's fine.

But the tech community isn't faceless. In fact, I would argue it's one of the only markets where your individual reputation can supersede an organizational reputation. There are more than a few examples of people who have a strong public reputation despite working for no-name companies. Do you know where Martin Kleppman's works? Probably not, but you've most likely have heard of Designing Data Intensive Applications. How about Fabrice Bellard? What's the name of his company?

I think you get my point. In short, your reputation matters.

I would argue that your reputation actually matters more now than ever before. When creation is cheap, the audience looks to those who have strong reputations for building with care and committment.

Simply creating a project isn't a signal of commitment anymore. Anyone can point an agent at something and says, "replicate this, make no mistakes".

Recently it was instinct.

Within a day or two, I saw 2-3 "open source instinct" projects being launched.
